## Overview

A quantitative study of peer-to-peer microfinance platforms operating in rural Indonesia, Vietnam, and the Philippines, assessing repayment rates, income effects, and long-run household welfare.

## Data & Methods

Panel data from three P2P platforms (2018–2022) was matched with household survey records. A difference-in-differences design estimates the causal effect of loan access on consumption smoothing.

## Findings

- Micro-loan access reduced consumption volatility by roughly 15% for recipient households.
- Group-lending models outperformed individual loans on repayment rates by 8–12 percentage points.
- Digital onboarding reduced operational costs enough to make sub-$200 loans economically viable.

## Limitations & Next Steps

Selection bias remains a concern — households that seek loans may be systematically different. Future work should use randomised credit lotteries to sharpen causal identification.
