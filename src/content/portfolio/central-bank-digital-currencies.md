## Overview

A survey of CBDC pilot programmes across 12 jurisdictions, examining design choices, monetary policy implications, and financial inclusion outcomes.

## Background

Over 130 countries are exploring or have launched CBDCs. The design spectrum ranges from account-based retail CBDCs (like China's e-CNY) to token-based wholesale systems for interbank settlement.

## Key Themes

- **Disintermediation risk**: direct central bank accounts could reduce commercial bank deposit bases.
- **Financial inclusion**: mobile-accessible CBDCs may reach unbanked populations more effectively than traditional accounts.
- **Programmability**: smart-contract-enabled CBDCs raise questions about monetary sovereignty and privacy.

## Conclusions

CBDC design is fundamentally a political economy problem as much as a technical one — the tradeoffs between programmability, privacy, and stability have no single optimal solution.
