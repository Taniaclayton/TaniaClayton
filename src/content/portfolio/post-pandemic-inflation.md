## Overview

A cross-country analysis of how supply-chain disruptions, fiscal stimulus, and energy price shocks combined to produce the highest inflation rates seen in the G7 since the 1980s.

## Methodology

Consumer price index data was sourced from OECD and national statistics agencies for 2019–2023. A decomposition approach separated demand-pull from cost-push drivers in each country.

## Key Findings

- Energy and food price shocks accounted for the majority of the initial CPI spike in 2021–2022.
- Labour market tightness amplified second-round effects in the US and UK more than in continental Europe.
- Monetary policy transmission lagged by an average of 12–18 months across the sample.

## Conclusions

The post-pandemic inflation episode underscores the limits of purely demand-side monetary frameworks when supply is the primary shock.
