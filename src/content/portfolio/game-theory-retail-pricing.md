## Overview

An applied case study examining how competing retailers use strategic pricing decisions — and how Nash equilibrium concepts explain the patterns we observe.

## Key Questions

- Why do petrol stations on the same intersection charge nearly identical prices?
- How do supermarket price-match guarantees function as credible commitment devices?
- When does a pricing war become a prisoner's dilemma?

## Analysis

Using publicly available pricing data from three retail categories, the study maps best-response functions and identifies equilibrium outcomes compared to collusive and competitive benchmarks.

## Conclusions

Strategic pricing in retail markets consistently mirrors theoretical predictions from repeated game models, suggesting firms implicitly solve for subgame-perfect equilibria over long time horizons.
