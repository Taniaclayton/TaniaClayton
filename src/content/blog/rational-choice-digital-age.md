Algorithmic nudges and social-media feedback loops are quietly redesigning what economists once treated as a stable primitive: the rational consumer. This post sketches where the standard model strains under digital conditions.

## The classical assumption

Rational choice theory assumes agents hold stable, transitive preferences and optimise over them given a budget constraint. It is elegant, tractable, and — increasingly — a poor description of behaviour shaped by recommendation engines.

## Where it breaks

When the choice architecture itself adapts in real time to maximise engagement, "preferences" become partly endogenous to the platform. Lorem ipsum dolor sit amet, the feedback loop is the point, not noise around it.

## What replaces it

A richer account borrows from behavioural economics — bounded rationality, salience, and habit formation — to model decisions as path-dependent rather than purely optimising.
