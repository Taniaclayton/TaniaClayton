Carbon credits promise a market solution to an externality. But the accounting behind them is doing a lot of quiet work, and the incentives don't always point where the headline numbers suggest.

## Additionality is hard

A credit is only meaningful if the abatement would not have happened anyway. Establishing that counterfactual is genuinely difficult, and the cheapest credits are often the least additional.

## The veneer problem

When offsets are cheaper than abatement, firms can buy their way to a "net zero" label without changing operations. That is efficient in a narrow sense and unhelpful in a broad one.
